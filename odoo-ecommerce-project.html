Odoo Ecommerce

An online store that looks like your brand, without hiring a developer

Small businesses on Odoo could sell online, but their stores looked generic, and anything more than a new logo needed a developer. I designed a store builder with finished templates, reusable sections and AI-written content, so owners can build something distinctive on their own.

01Overview

Project overview: before, turning point, after

This is the story of how small businesses on Odoo went from stores that looked like everyone else's to stores that look like their own brand, without hiring a developer. I designed the whole experience: the builder, AI-assisted customisation, dashboards, responsive layouts and the design system.

Before

Easy stores looked generic; custom ones needed a developer

Owners could change colours and a logo, then hit a wall. Anything more meant waiting for technical help, and writing all the content was the slowest part of launching.

The turning point

Owners wanted building blocks, not a blank page

They didn't want a blank canvas or a locked template. They wanted good pieces they could mix, restyle and fill with content quickly.

After

Polished pieces that always fit together

Finished templates, a library of reusable sections, rich shop pages and AI-written content, all built on one design system, so every combination still looks right.

02Context

Selling online on Odoo

Odoo connects a business's online store directly to its stock, orders and accounting, a big advantage for growing businesses. But making that store look and feel like a modern brand usually takes design and development time most small teams don't have.

Where it breaks down

The store is built in

Products, stock and orders already live in Odoo, so the shop stays up to date automatically.

Templates can feel generic

Many stores end up looking alike unless someone pays for custom design.

Bigger changes need developers

Changing layouts, sections or flows usually means asking for technical help.

Content takes time

Writing product descriptions, banners and page text is slow for a small team.

Key terms

Storefront
The part of an online store that shoppers see: home page, product pages, cart and checkout.
Template
A finished page design you start from and adapt, instead of starting from a blank page.
Section
A reusable block, like a banner, product grid or contact form, that you drop into any page.
Design system
One shared set of buttons, colours and styles that keeps every page consistent.
ERP
Software that runs a company's back office: stock, orders, accounting. Odoo is one.
No-code
Building something by choosing and arranging pieces on screen, without writing code.

Flexibility usually meant complexity

The goal was to give owners more freedom to customise, make setup simpler, and deliver a store that looks modern, all at the same time.

How might we help any business build a distinctive, modern store on Odoo, with templates, reusable sections and AI doing the heavy lifting?

What we found

Owners kept describing the same walls they hit. Paraphrased from the interviews:

“Beyond colours and the logo, I'm stuck.”

“My store looks exactly like my competitor's.”

“Shoppers filter by category, price and colour before anything else.”

“Every new page, I start again from scratch.”

“Writing the content takes longer than building the page.”

“When buttons and forms don't match, the store feels less trustworthy.”

Key insight

Owners didn't want a blank canvas or a locked template. They wanted good building blocks they could mix, restyle and fill with content quickly.

07Design Decisions

Why it works the way it does

The strategy: give owners a library of polished, consistent building blocks, and let AI fill them. Here's what we noticed, what we could have done, and what we chose.

Start from a finished store

We noticed: a blank page froze people. So owners pick from several complete home page designs and adapt one, which gives them a professional starting point from minute one.

Instead ofA blank page + a block editor
We choseComplete home page templates

Shop pages built for browsing

We noticed: shoppers narrow things down before they look closely. So shop pages come with category, price and colour filters, grid and list views, and labels like New, Hot and Sale to make big catalogues easy to scan.

Instead ofA simple list of products
We choseA filterable shop with visual labels

Sections, not pages

We noticed: owners rebuilt every new page from scratch. So contact sections, popups and other page parts are designed as interchangeable blocks, and new pages get assembled rather than rebuilt.

Instead ofOne-off page designs
We choseA library of reusable sections & popups

Quick, easy sign-up

We noticed: long sign-up forms lost shoppers. So login and sign-up pages offer “continue with Google”-style options and short forms, and still match the brand.

Instead ofLong registration forms
We choseSocial sign-in + short forms

A design system underneath everything

We noticed: mismatched buttons and forms made stores feel less trustworthy. So buttons, inputs, toggles, checkboxes, ratings, labels and tooltips all come from one shared system, and every template and section looks consistent however owners combine them.

Instead ofSeparate styles for each template
We choseOne shared component library
The hardest part

Giving owners real freedom without letting them break the design. The answer was to make the pieces flexible, but the rules underneath them firm.

Building blocks for distinctive stores

Owners build professional stores from finished templates, reusable sections, smart shop pages and AI-written content, all fitted to their own business.

10Reflection

What this project taught me

Odoo Ecommerce turned store setup from a technical job into something a founder can do alone. Pairing Odoo's selling tools with simple, well-made building blocks gave both small shops and larger businesses a store that's flexible and easy to grow.

Lesson 01

Constraints enable creativity

A strong set of components gave owners more freedom, not less, because everything they combined still looked right.

Lesson 02

Templates are a starting line

Finished designs made launching easier; reusable sections kept stores growing without falling apart.

Lesson 03

Use AI on the slowest step

Writing content, not arranging layouts, was the real bottleneck, so that's where AI help mattered most.
